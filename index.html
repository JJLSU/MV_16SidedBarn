Grain, straw, and chaff

Grain: the edible wheat kernels.
Straw: the long stems left after threshing.
Chaff: the light husks and other small plant fragments mixed with the grain.

Treading loosens the grain. Winnowing separates lighter chaff from heavier grain. This model shows treading and collection.

 A CLEVER BUILDING. REAL PEOPLE.

Why sixteen sides?

The almost-round shape gave horses a path to trot around. Upstairs, their hooves loosened wheat kernels, which dropped through narrow gaps into the clean room below. The roof kept rain out. This was treading, a form of threshing. After gathering the grain downstairs, people removed the lighter chaff by winnowing. That later cleaning step is not simulated here.

Enslaved people built and operated the barn, tended the horses, and gathered the grain. They were forced to work and were not free to choose their lives. Their knowledge and labor made this system work.

The original stood at Dogue Run Farm. The barn visitors see at Mount Vernon today is a replica. This model uses the documented 52-foot diameter, sixteen outer walls, eight inner framing posts, barred lower windows, and a separate ramp entrance. Siding and door construction follow photographs of the reconstructed barn. Heights, door locations beyond the photographed entrance, terrain, and textures remain interpretive. Half walls are a teaching view, not the original construction. The process is sped up.
